========================================================================
GETTING STARTED: Build a Calculator with Claude (In-Class Group Activity)
========================================================================

A 30-minute group activity. Your group of three builds a working Python
calculator with Claude as your coding partner. You do not need to know
how to code.

FILES IN THIS FOLDER
  Claude_Calculator_Group_Activity.pdf   The activity handout. Open this
                                         one if you can.
  Claude_Calculator_Group_Activity.docx  The same handout as a Word
                                         document.
  GETTING_STARTED.txt                    This file.

GET THE FILES ONTO YOUR COMPUTER
If you are looking at this on GitHub, you need to download the files
before you can use them:
  - One file: click the file name, then click the download button
    (the small arrow icon labeled "Download raw file") near the top right.
  - Everything at once: go to the main page of the repository, click the
    green "Code" button, then "Download ZIP". Unzip it on your computer
    (on Windows: right-click > Extract All; on Mac: double-click it).

STEP 1: OPEN THE HANDOUT
Open Claude_Calculator_Group_Activity.pdf (or the .docx version). It has
the timeline, your group roles, the prompts to use, and a Test Log to
fill in. Your instructor may hand out printed copies.

STEP 2: GET READY BEFORE THE ACTIVITY STARTS
  - Form a group of 3 and pick roles: Prompter, Coder, and
    Tester/Recorder (the handout explains each one).
  - Make sure at least one person in the group can log in to replit.com
    and claude.ai.
  - Have a pen ready for the Test Log.

STEP 3: FOLLOW THE HANDOUT
The handout walks you through creating the Replit project, so start at
its "Step 1: Set Up". There are no starter files: Claude writes all the
code with you.


IF YOU GET STUCK
  - Check the "Tips for Working with Claude" box in the handout.
  - If the calculator crashes, that's part of the activity: copy the red
    error message and use the handout's "Prompt 3 (fixing bugs)".
  - Still stuck? Raise your hand and ask your instructor.
